Weightlifting & Strength Training

Topic Description

Weightlifting and strength training are forms of exercise that use resistance to improve physical strength and fitness. People can use weightlifting to build muscle, increase strength, improve bone density, and improve their overall health. I personally work out two hours a day, six days a week, and my entire focus is strength training. I believe that the benefits of different forms of training are incredible, both physically and mentally, but strength training is particularly rewarding. However, getting started with weightlifting can be confusing because there are many different exercises, muscle groups, and training methods to learn. My web application will help make this information easier to understand for beginners by allowing users to explore different muscle groups and the exercises associated with them, as well as introductory knowledge about training.

There are already several organizations that provide information about weightlifting and strength training. The National Strength and Conditioning Association (NSCA), for example, provides a large amount of reliable information and educational material about strength and conditioning. However, the amount and depth of the information available can be overwhelming for someone who is just beginning to learn about weightlifting. The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) also provides valuable exercise and fitness resources, but much of its website is directed toward memberships, certifications, professional education, and resources for people pursuing careers in the field. My application will take a simpler approach by focusing specifically on beginners who want to learn the basics of strength training. Users will be able to easily explore exercises by muscle group while also learning fundamental concepts such as muscle growth, progressive overload, training volume, recovery, and common weightlifting terminology.

App Description

Home Page

The Home page will introduce users to weightlifting and strength training and explain the purpose of the application. It will contain a brief introduction to weightlifting and why training is important, 2-3 images related to strength training for visual purposes, and an overview of the different features available on the website. The page will also provide navigation to the Exercise Explorer, Information Page, and the Contact Us page.

Exercise Explorer

This is the interactive part of the application. The first step will let users choose between five muscle groups: chest, back, legs, shoulders, and arms. From there, the second step will provide users with at least four different exercises that target the chosen muscle group. Once an exercise is selected, there will be an anatomical model similar to those seen at a gym, with the muscles used during the exercise highlighted. There will also possibly be a short GIF of me demonstrating the exercise if I am able to implement it. In addition, the page will include a short description of how to perform the exercise, as well as important cues for feeling the exercise in the targeted muscle.

Information Page

The Information page will provide users with general information about weightlifting, strength training, and nutrition. It will have sections that teach beginners important terminology, such as reps, sets, compound and isolation lifts, volume versus intensity, 1RM, RPE, and other common terms. It will also cover progressive overload and its importance in strength training. Beyond the actual weightlifting, this page will discuss nutrition and recovery and how they relate to making progress in the gym.

Contact Us Page

The Contact Us page will allow users to ask questions and provide feedback about the website. Users will be able to enter their name and email, as well as ask any training questions they may have about weightlifting or strength training. The page will also give users the option to provide feedback or suggestions for the website. Common questions and answers that I receive through the Contact Us page will also be displayed on the page so that future users can easily find answers to questions that other users have already asked.
